# Major U.S. equity drawdown turning points in the ES sample (1982–2024)

This note maps prominent S&P 500 drawdown troughs within the period represented by
the long ES research series to their historical context. It is an event-annotation
guide for chart and strategy review, not a claim that a single news event caused an
exact market low.

## Interpretation

The dates below refer to the cash S&P 500's commonly cited closing troughs or, where
appropriate, a short turning-point window. The research ES series is a continuous,
adjusted futures series, so its prices and local extrema can differ from the cash
index because of futures rolls, adjustment methodology, trading calendar, and
sampling. Use these as date windows to inspect in ES, not as exact price-level
matches. The available sample starts in September 1982, after the major 1980–82
equity bear market had already bottomed.

## Major drawdowns and turning points

| Trough / window | Context | Classification / note |
|---|---|---|
| **May 31, 1984** | The market was in a May–June slide amid rising interest rates and stress around Continental Illinois, which faced a depositor run and a federal rescue announced May 17. | A local ES-series turn/date of interest, not a major S&P bear-market trough; the S&P's broader decline continued into June/July. The S&P fell about 6% from early May through mid-June. [Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco review](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/economic-letter-federal-reserve-bank-san-francisco-4960/market-responses-continental-illinois-517590/fulltext), [Federal Reserve history of Continental Illinois](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/continental-illinois) |
| **Oct–Dec 1987**; cash S&P trough **Dec 4** | Black Monday on Oct 19 was the acute crash, amid market-structure stress and program/portfolio-insurance selling. The market continued falling after crash day. | Major bear market; do not mark Oct 19 as the final low. [Federal Reserve history](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/stock-market-crash-of-1987), [trough chronology](https://historyofmarket.com/sp500/bear-markets/1987/) |
| **Oct 11, 1990** | Recession fears and the oil-price shock following Iraq's invasion of Kuwait. | Near-bear correction: about 19.9% peak-to-trough, just short of the conventional 20% threshold. [Market chronology](https://www.getswoopr.com/market-history/1990-91-gulf-war-oil-shock-1990/) |
| **Aug 1998**; initial cash-index low **Aug 31** | Asian financial crisis, Russia's debt default/devaluation, and stress around Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM). | Sharp correction and volatility spike, rather than a prolonged bear market. [Federal Reserve history on LTCM](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/ltcm-near-failure), [drawdown chronology](https://tideline.jbpscapital.com/market-data/sp500-bear-markets) |
| **Oct 9, 2002** | Dot-com valuation collapse, the 2001 recession, 9/11, and corporate-accounting failures including Enron and WorldCom. | Major, multi-year bear market. [Federal Reserve remarks on the bubble and recession](https://www.federalreserve.gov/boarddocs/speeches/2002/20021015/), [trough chronology](https://trendfollowing.com/pdfs/sp.pdf) |
| **Nov 20, 2008 (volatility extreme)** | During the Global Financial Crisis selloff, the S&P 500 fell 6.71% and the VIX closed at 80.86, then a record. | A panic/volatility peak within the bear market, not its trough; the S&P 500 continued lower into March 2009. A roughly 70% annualized historical point-vol estimate for this date is plausible: with 256-day annualization it represents about 4.4% daily volatility. This smoothed realized estimate is not directly comparable to VIX's option-implied 30-day volatility. [CBOE historical discussion](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/documents/impliedcorrelationindicator.pdf) |
| **Mar 9, 2009** | Global financial crisis: housing/mortgage losses spread through financial institutions and credit markets, followed by recession. | Major bear-market trough. [Federal Reserve history](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/great-recession-of-200709) |
| **Early Oct 2011** | U.S. debt-ceiling confrontation and credit-rating downgrade overlapped with the euro-area sovereign-debt crisis. | Severe correction; classification varies with intraday versus closing-price convention. |
| **Feb 11, 2016** | China-growth and currency concerns, a steep oil/commodity slump, and broader global-demand worries. | Correction/volatility trough, not a conventional 20% S&P bear market. [Contemporaneous market report](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-02-10/dollar-nurses-losses-on-yellen-rate-comments-oil-back-below-28) |
| **Dec 24, 2018** | Fed tightening, U.S.–China trade tensions, and slowing-growth fears. | Near-bear correction: the S&P 500 decline was about 19.8%. [Bear-market chronology and definition](https://www.kiplinger.com/slideshow/investing/t052-s001-8-facts-you-need-to-know-about-bear-markets/index.html) |
| **Mar 23, 2020** | COVID-19 shutdowns and acute market/liquidity stress. | Fast, deep bear market; the reversal followed extraordinary policy support and a rapid shift in expectations. [Bear-market dates](https://historyofmarket.com/sp500/bear-markets/2022/) |
| **Oct 12, 2022** | High inflation and rapid Federal Reserve rate increases; Russia's invasion of Ukraine amplified energy and supply uncertainty. | Bear-market trough. [AP overview and dates](https://apnews.com/article/c5d106a80666c0ff4f0e71624d525158) |

## Suggested chart annotations

For a first visual review, annotate **May–July 1984** (with May 31 as a local ES
turn to inspect), **1987, 1990, 1998, 2000–02, 2007–09, 2011,
2015–16, 2018, 2020, and 2022**. The 1990, 2011, and 2018 episodes are useful
examples of large selloffs that sit near the conventional 20% bear-market boundary;
they should not all be treated as equivalent to the 2000–02 or 2007–09 prolonged
bears.

When comparing signals and positions, inspect both the decline's onset and the
trough/recovery window. A strategy can react very differently to a slow valuation
unwind (2000–02), a financial-system crisis (2007–09), and a fast shock/rebound
(2020); one trough date alone hides that distinction.
